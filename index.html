About Me

I investigate how semantic knowledge and symbolic reasoning can make AI systems more explainable, reliable, and scientifically useful.

I am a Research Associate at the TIB – Leibniz Information Centre for Science and Technology in Hannover, Germany, and a Ph.D. researcher working at the intersection of knowledge graphs, explainable AI, causal reasoning, and neuro-symbolic artificial intelligence.

My doctoral thesis, “Explainable Causal Models on top of Cognitive Knowledge Graphs,” studies how structured knowledge, symbolic constraints, and causal representations can complement data-driven models. The goal is to improve transparency, traceability, and trust in complex decision-making systems.

My work spans knowledge graph completion, link-prediction explanations, interpretable machine learning, semantic descriptions of AI pipelines, symbolic chain-of-thought generation, and hybrid AI applications in medicine.

Research Agenda

From Data to Explainable Knowledge

Research Challenge

Modern machine-learning models often produce strong predictions without revealing the semantic evidence, causal structure, or reasoning path behind their decisions.

Research Direction

I combine cognitive knowledge graphs with symbolic and causal models to represent background knowledge, validate predictions, and generate human-understandable explanations.

Expected Impact

The resulting hybrid systems aim to be more transparent, logically coherent, reusable, and suitable for high-stakes domains where trust and traceability matter.

Current Topics

Rule-guided multi-hop reasoning, explainable link prediction, symbolic chain-of-thoughts, semantic AI-pipeline documentation, and neuro-symbolic knowledge graph completion.
